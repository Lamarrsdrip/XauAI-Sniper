### Honest assessment — the fix worked exactly as diagnosed, but it surfaces a real risk/reward problem

The fix did precisely what the root-cause analysis predicted: trade count went from 6 to 39 in the identical 7-day window, proving the bug really was suppressing ~85% of already-approved M30 candidates. **This is not a new bug introduced by the fix — it is the M30 architecture's actual behavior at its intended trade frequency, which the original bug had been accidentally hiding.**

That real behavior is concerning: at scale, **average losses ($1,050.81) are roughly 3.4x average wins ($307.59)**. A 76.9% win rate is not enough to overcome that ratio — the math is `0.769 × 307.59 − 0.231 × 1050.81 ≈ -8` per trade, consistent with the observed -$5.88 expected payoff. Several individual losses (-$1,332.42, -$1,320.00, -$1,231.56, -$1,152.13) are real broker-confirmed stop-loss hits (`sl` price shown matches the fill), not profit-floor ratchets — a materially different pattern from every smaller/luckier sample window examined earlier tonight.

**This is exactly the outcome the owner's protocol warned against and explicitly separated from bug-fixing: do not celebrate an artificially attractive backtest, and do not assume higher activity alone is an improvement.** The bug fix is correct and should stand (it removes a real hidden cooldown the spec forbids) — but the resulting higher-frequency behavior needs its own honest evaluation, not an assumption that "more trades = better."
